01Selected work

Four projects, and the decision inside each one.

Two have real captures. Two are drawn as diagrams because they are not finished, and I would rather show the architecture than fake a screen.

No. 01Open source

MetalVisualKit

A Swift package of GPU-backed SwiftUI views: a compute-shader particle loader and an ARKit LiDAR point-cloud renderer that samples the camera image per point. SwiftUI owns the view lifecycle; Metal owns the per-point work.

Keep the public API as SwiftUI views, not Metal objects. Someone using the package should never need to meet a compute encoder.

No. 02Public repo

DeviceMonitor

My degree project: a network-device dashboard with status history, filtering and a detail view, built around three boundaries — a mock service behind a protocol, SwiftData storage with actor-safe fetch and save, and SwiftUI views observing one view model.

Mock data behind a protocol: the point is to demonstrate the architecture, not the network.

From the case study

Swift 6 · SwiftUI · SwiftData · XCTest · XCUITest · GitHub ActionsGitHub Case study

No. 03In development

Subtl

Captioning for short-form video with transcription on the device, so nothing leaves the phone. Preview and export read the same caption-style model, which is what stops the exported file drifting from what you edited. The support site and privacy policy are live; the app is in development.

One caption-style model drives both preview and export, so nothing changes after export.

No. 04Experimental

Prism Spatial

Rendering research into Gaussian splats and spatial capture. When RealityKit's abstractions got in the way of projection and depth sorting, I wrote a custom Metal path instead, which is where most of the learning has been.

Rescope when the framework abstraction becomes the constraint: a custom Metal path, not RealityKit.
