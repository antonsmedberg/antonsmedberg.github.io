Prism Spatial

Rendering research into Gaussian splats and spatial capture. When RealityKit's abstractions got in the way of projection and depth sorting, I wrote a custom Metal path instead, which is where most of the learning has been.

What it does

Prism Spatial imports Gaussian-splat captures in PLY form, parses them on the CPU and sorts the splats by depth each frame, then draws them with a custom Metal renderer that owns the projection and the splat rasterisation. The result is presented through a CAMetalLayer. Tilt-shift and colour grading are part of the presentation concept rather than effects bolted on afterwards, and SwiftData handles local persistence.

The decision

Rescope when the framework abstraction becomes the constraint: a custom Metal path, not RealityKit.

The project started as a rendering experiment, not a product, and the first version used RealityKit. Splats need their own projection and a back-to-front order every frame, and those are exactly the parts the framework abstracts away. Rather than force the whole project around that constraint, I rescoped: the Metal renderer makes projection and rasterisation explicit, and the depth sort happens on the CPU before the draw. That path is where most of the learning has been.

What I would do differently

The depth sort runs on the CPU, per frame. That is the simplest way to get a correct image and the first thing I would change, because it is the first bottleneck a splat renderer meets once captures grow. Moving the sort to the GPU, or reusing the previous frame's order when the camera barely moves, is the next step.

Current state

Experimental and unreleased, with no public repository. The rendering path above is drawn, not captured.
