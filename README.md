# antonsmedberg.github.io

I'm Anton Smedberg, an iOS developer in Malmö with a focus on UI and UX. This repository is my portfolio, live at [antonsmedberg.github.io](https://antonsmedberg.github.io/).

I'm looking for a first junior role in iOS, Android or Java, in Skåne, Copenhagen or remote. I work in Swedish and English.

## Running it

Static HTML, CSS and JavaScript. No framework, no build step, no `npm install`. GitHub Pages serves `main` as it is.

```bash
python3 -m http.server 8000
```

## Layout

```
index.html                 English home page
sv/index.html              Swedish home page, written in Swedish rather than translated
cv/, sv/cv/                CV pages, built from assets/data/cv.json
work/*.html                Case studies: Subtl, Giggle Dash, DeviceMonitor, MetalVisualKit
work/img/                  Project screenshots and app icons, built by tools/build-images.sh
subtl/                     Subtl support site and privacy policy, with its own stylesheet
assets/css/style.css       Tokens, type, the glass material, controls, the bar and the app window
assets/css/home.css        The home screen: the name, the almanac, the sun's room and the apps, at every size of window
assets/css/sky.css         What the weather writes into the almanac, loaded with it
assets/css/case-study.css  Case studies, which are also what a project's window shows, and the 404
assets/css/cv.css          CV pages: the section index, the timeline, the skill chips, the print layout
assets/js/main.js          Springs, swiped pages, the bar, the app window, the sun as the clock, the CV index
assets/js/sky.js           The forecast's symbols, loaded with the weather
assets/js/weather.js       Malmö's weather from SMHI, fetched after the page has loaded
assets/js/city.js          The 3D Malmö, rendered in pixels with WebGL, with its sun and moon
assets/js/city-sky.js      The city's clouds, rain, snow, lightning and water, loaded after the city's first frames
assets/js/smed3d.js        The mascot, a small lamplighter, drawn live as 3D pixel art
assets/art/                Section marks, the city still shown before the city starts, a still of the mascot
assets/img/icons.svg       Every icon, in one sprite
assets/fonts/              Bricolage Grotesque, Inter and their licences
assets/cv/                 CV PDFs, English and Swedish
assets/data/cv.json        CV content for the CV pages

tools/art/build-objects.py Bakes project objects in the mascot's shaders (Home's MetalVisualKit ring)
tools/build-cv.py          Builds cv/ and sv/cv/ from cv.json
tools/build-images.sh      Builds every raster image from the project checkouts
tools/stamp.py             Writes the build date and page weight into the pages
tools/source/              Portrait cutout, Giggle Dash captures
```

## Rebuilding

```bash
python3 tools/build-cv.py     # CV pages from assets/data/cv.json
tools/build-images.sh         # screenshots, icons, profile picture, link preview
python3 tools/art/sprites.py  # the MetalVisualKit tile
python3 tools/stamp.py        # build date, request count and page weight
```

`build-images.sh` needs `sips`, `cwebp`, `ffmpeg` and Chrome. It reads the screenshots straight from the Subtl, DeviceMonitor, MetalVisualKit and Giggle Dash checkouts. It expects them next to this repository, or you can pass the folder that holds them. The link preview is the cover I made for LinkedIn, scaled and cut to 1200 by 630 and saved as a JPEG. The cover is composed outside this repository and kept in `Media/`, which Git ignores, so without it the script leaves the committed picture alone.

The city and the mascot have no build step: `assets/js/city.js` and `assets/js/smed3d.js` draw them in the browser. Where WebGL 2 is missing, Bit is one still picture, `assets/art/mascot-still.webp`, and it loads only then. Until the city has drawn its first frame, the screen is a calm gradient for the hour: a small script in the page picks night, twilight or day in Malmö from rough sunrise and sunset times by month, before the first paint, and sets the words' ink to match. The city then fades in over it in half a second. The city's own still, `assets/art/malmo-still.webp`, the clear night at 22:00 captured from the city with the page hidden, is fetched only where WebGL 2 is missing. The four section marks in `assets/art/` (Experience, Skills, About and CV) are drawn as app icons and stored at 60, 120, 180 and 240 pixels. They head the sections of the CV page, cut to the same squircle as the icons of the apps. Their drawing script is not in this repository yet.

## Decisions I'd defend

- **The scene is the interface.** The portfolio is a home screen, and the city fills it. On it stand my name, one line about me with a small picture, the sun and one compact row of four small app tiles, written straight on the sky and the water with no card under them. Home, Work, CV and Contact are pages you swipe between. The four apps open in place from their tiles and keep their real addresses, the line about me opens About, and everything is still a plain link. Nothing is painted behind the words and the picture is not darkened for them. The page tells the city where its words end at the top and where the apps begin at the foot, and the city says which ink each of those zones needs: cream where the picture behind is deep, navy where it is bright, as on a clear noon. The words change ink in one soft move when the light does. Each ink keeps a contrast of 4.5:1 against the darkest or brightest picture the city lets stand behind it.
- **The sun is the clock.** In the sky left of the tower stands the sun, where the sun over Malmö stands on its way from sunrise to sunset. The city draws it there, a small white disc in a soft bloom that warms and widens as it sinks, with its light on the water. By night the moon makes the same crossing in its real phase. The page only says where they stand, and keeps a handle there. Take hold of the sun, or focus it and use the arrow keys, and its path comes out as a hairline with a tick at sunrise, noon and sunset; it follows the finger and runs the city's day and its weather, and let go, it springs back to now. Add `?hour=16` to the address to start at another hour.
- **The time and the weather are written on the sky.** The almanac has no frame and nothing behind it, only three lines. A small line in spaced capitals says what it shows: Malmö now, or how many hours ahead while the sun is held. Then Malmö's time, set light in the system face with even figures, as a lock screen sets it. Then one line with the hour's symbol, temperature and weather in a word. It has a colour of its own, never the name's: lamplight gold on a dark sky, deep ultramarine on a bright one. The symbols are drawn in one stroke, as tall as the figures beside them. On a phone the almanac stands under the line about me; on a computer it stands apart on the sky's open side, under the header's capsule. Numbers change without moving anything around them, and nothing moves for a reader who asks for less motion. While the sun is held the almanac shows that hour and its forecast, up to a day ahead, and the city draws its clouds, rain, snow and fog. The weather is SMHI's forecast for Malmö. `?wx=clear`, `cloudy`, `overcast`, `rain`, `snow`, `fog` or `thunder` shows a state without asking SMHI, and with `?hour=` nothing is asked unless `?wx=live` is given too.
- **One layout that follows the space it gets.** The grids answer to the width of their container, so the same CV is one column in a phone-sized window and two in a wide one. A short window, or a phone on its side, turns the bottom bar into a rail down the side.
- **A real 3D Malmö, drawn in pixels.** The wallpaper is a small WebGL scene rendered at low resolution. One art pixel is always a whole number of screen pixels. It has the Turning Torso with its steel spine and the Öresund Bridge with its harp cables. Around them are Västra Hamnen with its boardwalk, Ribersborgs Kallbadhus, a skyline and the Lillgrund wind turbines. A train and cars cross the bridge, boats and gulls move, a flag flies at the pier head and cyclists pass on the promenade. The moving things are sent to the graphics card as the page loads, and after that a few numbers a frame move each one. The town is drawn again only when the view, the light or the weather on it changes. Where there is time for it, the town is drawn at twice the picture's resolution. Each art pixel is then the mean of what it covers. Sky, water and weather are painted round it more finely, at up to two device pixels per CSS pixel. The picture is composed in layers. The pier, the quay and the tower, the skyline and the far shore each stand a step further into the air. Clouds are heaps in a layer of air, lit on their crowns and shaded below. Each frame marches through them from a new start and is averaged with up to fifteen before it. Their edges come out soft and clean. Every hour has one palette, and the words get one key, light or dark. Swiping moves a camera through it. The shapes follow photos and published dimensions, but the distances are compressed to fit a phone. The sun's height and the sky follow the real time of day in Malmö.
- **The pages are one space.** What a page holds lies at different depths. On the way between two pages the far layers lag and the near ones run ahead, by a translate only: a scale, a turn or a fade would take the frost off their glass. The city's camera rises and pulls back on the same trip, turning from the tower toward the bridge. All of it is a function of how far the pages have scrolled, so it follows a finger exactly. The pointer, or a phone that reports its tilt without asking, slides the city's planes as if the camera stepped a few metres aside. It also drifts the home screen's layers against the city. At rest the planes drift a little on their own. Under an open project the home screen stays where it is and a scrim dims it. With Reduce Motion on, pages fade in and nothing travels.
- **The mascot lives on the water.** He is a small lamplighter in a yellow oilskin coat and a sou'wester who rows a wooden boat across Home's water. His lantern hangs from a curved pole at the bow. He is pixel art, drawn live from a 3D figure, with a one-pixel outline and a few shades of each colour. A new pose comes 15 times a second. His water is a lane between the app tiles and the bar. He rows across it and back, facing the stern as one rows, and drifts with his oars on the water and looks about. Now and then he waves and the boat wobbles, a fish jumps over an oar, or he raises his lantern to look. In rain he tips the water off his brim. Tap him and he waves and says hello. The city's sun lights him. His lantern lights up in steps as dusk falls, with a short broken streak of light on the water under it. Late at night he nods off where he floats. He belongs to Home: he goes with it when another tab opens and is paused while it is out of sight. Under an open window he shrinks away, and he comes back when it closes. His file is asked for only once the city has started, and he rows in from the edge.
- **The whole name is readable.** My name stands on one line from 360 pixels wide, on two below that, at 38 pixels on a phone and 60 to 72 on a computer, so the city keeps most of the window. Where a letter meets the Turning Torso, the city repaints the tower's own pixels on a second canvas above the text, so the overlap reads as depth and no letter is hidden.
- **One frosted glass for every surface.** The header's pills, the bar, the page heads, the project and CV cards, the Contact card, the chips and the quiet buttons are one material, so they melt together with the city. It is a tint in the page's light, and the page's light follows the scene as the city measures it, not the hour's name: light frost under navy ink while the picture behind is bright, a bright dusk included, dark frost under cream ink once it is dark, never a dark and a light surface on one screen. Once the page is up, a new key must hold for three seconds while nothing moves before the light turns, and the surfaces cross-fade over a second, so it never flips while someone reads. Behind the tint the scene is blurred with a little saturation and never darkened or lifted; the tint alone carries the text, and its share was chosen by measuring the contrast of every line on the real scene on both sides of each turn, at 4.5:1 or more. Each surface has one rim, a hairline lit from above, and one soft shadow, all on its own box. A surface inside another is a tint, not a second blur. Nothing that blurs is ever under a scale, since Safari then draws the blur in the wrong place: buttons press by dropping a pixel, the window, which grows by a scale, is the frost made solid, and nothing on the pages is scaled or faded as they travel. Where the browser cannot blur, or the reader asks for less transparency or more contrast, the frost is nearly solid. The main action on a screen keeps its ultramarine fill with one soft highlight and one shadow of its own colour; a secondary action is a frost capsule, an icon-only control a circle, a container a rounded rectangle. Every control presses by dropping a pixel and darkening, shows a ring for a keyboard and lifts on hover where there is a pointer, and a tap is answered at once, with no wait for a double tap. Tags and dates are one chip, 28 pixels high. The CV's section chips and the bar mark the selected one the same way, with a drop of the accent's tint under full ink.
- **Scroll edges.** A page's text never runs readable under the header's pills or the bar: on the way to them it passes into a band of soft blur that deepens toward the edge. The band blurs and gathers the frost toward the edge, so the bottom one ends in the frost made solid under the bar and nothing of the page shows below it. The top one comes in as the page leaves its top, so at rest the sky behind the header stays sharp. In the app window, which is solid, the page fades into the window's own colour instead, and the CV's section chips sit in the window's top band, so nothing scrolls under them.
- **A small set of shapes.** Cards are squircles: paper, glass, a project, an app tile, a screenshot in its frame. Every control is a capsule, 36, 44 or 52 pixels tall. A dot is a state, such as the green one in the header. An app's icon is one layer: the picture cut to the squircle, one shadow and one hairline of light, with no plate behind it. It turns a few degrees toward the pointer, or with a tilted phone. The list is at the top of `assets/css/style.css`.
- **One header.** On the left the mark, in one colour, with a line of status over a line of platforms and one small green dot that does not move. On the right, language and theme share one glass capsule in two segments. On the home screen at rest the left group stands on the sky with no glass under it, in line with my name. On any other page it becomes a glass capsule. The header and the bar keep their full size and their names on every page at every scroll.
- **One line about me.** Under the name there is a small picture of me, a cutout standing in a tile of the sky's colours with a crisp side under it, so my whole head rises above the tile. Beside it are my role and one short line. The longer text lives in About, which the line opens. I had the portrait in a glass card of its own before. It was a good picture and one card too many.
- **A bar of my own.** The bottom bar is a rounded rectangle, not a copy of the iOS capsule. Its selection is a drop of brighter tint, with no rim or glass of its own, that lies inside the bar with the same 6 pixel gap on every side, and its corner is the bar's less that gap, so the two outlines run parallel. It goes to a tab on the pages' own spring, with no overshoot. Its tail follows, so the shape stretches on the way and runs together where it lands. It takes taps only; the pages themselves take the swipes. Its place is counted in tabs. In a short window the same bar stands as a rail.
- **Everything that moves is a spring.** Tabs, the window that projects open in, the rows of screens and the sun are stepped by one exact spring function, so a new touch can take any of them over mid-way. There are four springs in all: a quick one with a little overshoot for a control that answers a touch, one for a window on its way, a snappy one with no bounce that carries the pages and the bar's selection to a tab in about a third of a second and a window back to where it came from, and a calm one for what trails or returns. CSS transitions take 0.2 seconds for a fade, 0.4 for a press and 0.5 for a spring, and the layers between two pages slide only a little, more the nearer they are. The spring that moves the pages tells the layers, the city's camera and the sun where it is in the same frame, and nothing else starts until 300 milliseconds after it has landed. A swipe moves the pages under the finger once it has gone 7 pixels more across than down, painted once a frame; let go, and half a page or a throw sends them on, with the throw's speed carried into the spring. Up and down stays the page's own scroll, and a sideways wheel or trackpad turns one page per gesture. A project opens out of its tile or card and goes back into it. On Home each app is a 54 pixel tile with the continuous corner: its real icon, or for MetalVisualKit, whose icon is a blocky 30 pixel ring, the ring baked in Bit's own shaders on a navy ground. A tile is one box: its picture, one soft highlight and one hairline are layers of its own background and shadow under its one corner, so nothing can draw a second outline beside it, and it casts no mirror, which read as a doubled tile. A press drops it a pixel and darkens it like a key; on a computer, pointing at a tile or reaching it by key shows the first sentence of its card on Work under the row. The row keeps to the left, so the skyline and the tower's reflection stay free. A press presses a tile in and a mouse lifts it. Drag the window down by its top bar, or by the page when it is at its top: it follows the finger, shrinks a little and rounds its corners, and the pulled distance plus the throw decides whether it leaves. Leaving, it goes back in one straight motion on one spring that carries the throw on, aimed each frame at where the tile stands. The page shrinks with it and the two fade over the last third, while the tile, which never left its place, gives a small settle. Opening is the same in reverse, out of the tile with its corner. A window let go of short of leaving goes home on the throw with no bounce, its corners easing back as it comes. Every frame of it is a transform and opacities; the corner is never repainted over a page. Back, Esc and the browser's swipe-back close it too. With Reduce Motion on, nothing slides: a page or a window fades in or out over 150 milliseconds, a dragged window lets go with the same fade, and everything else jumps to its end state.
- **Projects are a deck.** On the Work page each project is a card in its own hue with its screens first. The cards stick as you scroll, so the next one slides over the last. Colours are OKLCH ramps on one hue per project, which keeps light and dark themes in step. Left to itself the page takes its light from Malmö's sky, which the home screen shows: paper by day and at dawn, dark at dusk and by night, so no card glares on the night city; a page that has not seen the sky follows the system, and the theme button overrides both. The Contact card is the page's own paper, not its inverse.
- **A CV made for a phone.** The CV has an index that follows you, a rail through jobs and schools, and skills as chips under iOS, Android and Java. The chips are cut from the sentences in `cv.json`. The sentences themselves stay in the page for print and for screen readers.
- **Real screens only.** Every image of an app is a capture of it running in the iOS Simulator. Screens are shown bare with rounded corners, because Apple's rules on device frames allow only its own bezels. The Subtl case study has one image built with those.
- **Light and dark from the system, with a toggle.** A small inline script applies a stored choice before first paint, so nothing flashes.
- **Numbers are measured.** The request count and page weight in the footers of the CV and the case studies are read from the files on disk by `tools/stamp.py`; the home screen shows none of them, only the weather credit and the source link at the foot of the Contact card. Its list is what a phone at three pixels to the point fetches for the first view, and it stops at 450 000 bytes. It also prints what comes after the page has loaded, the sun's art and the weather, on lines of their own.
- **The first picture needs three files.** The stylesheets and the display face are asked for first, and the face is preloaded; the sky behind the words is a gradient the page paints itself. The city is asked for once that picture is on the screen, and the mascot is fetched beside it and started after it, when the page is idle. The shader glass is fetched after the city's first frame; until then every shape is the stylesheet's glass. The marks of the CV's sections and the pixel face are not fetched until a page that shows them is opened. The weather comes after the page has loaded, and the city's clouds, rain, snow and water half a second after its first frames. Until the display face is in, its stand-in is Arial Bold at the same width and height, so a late swap moves nothing. Canvases stop when hidden, off screen or under a window. Links to my other pages are prefetched when a pointer rests on them, where the browser has speculation rules. No page has an unload handler, so the back and forward cache can keep it. GitHub Pages sets its own cache headers, ten minutes when I last looked, so the files keep plain names and nothing counts on a long cache; on a host where headers can be set, the files under `assets/` should get a hash in their names and a year.
- **One call to someone else, for the weather.** There is no CDN, no analytics and no Google Fonts. The one request that leaves this site goes to SMHI, Sweden's weather service, for Malmö's forecast. It is made after the page has loaded and asks for the forecast's address only. The browser adds its usual headers, among them the Origin, which names this site, but it sends no Referer and no cookie. The answer is kept for half an hour in the tab. If SMHI is slow or away, the almanac keeps to the clock and the sun. The weather is SMHI's open data under CC BY 4.0, and I draw it in my own symbols.
- **Type and space.** Two typefaces are served from this repository. Bricolage Grotesque sets my name and the headings. Small labels are the text face in tracked capitals. Inter sets body text where the device has no system font of its own, so Apple devices never fetch it. The type scale has eight steps: 11, 13, 15 and 17 pixels for text and four fluid sizes for display. Only my name on the home screen is outside it, because it is fitted to the width it gets. Every heading in the display face is set at the name's weight, 640, and small labels at 650 in capitals, so the tabs, the windows and the case studies read as one family with the home screen. Running text on a card is 17 pixels at half again its height. Tracking tightens as the type grows, headings are never hyphenated, and running text keeps to about 65 characters a line. Spacing is in steps of 4 pixels, every card has one padding, 24 pixels on a phone and 32 on a larger screen, controls are 36, 44 or 52 pixels high, and a shape inside another takes the outer corner less the gap between them. The icons are my own, one stroked family on a 24 pixel grid.
